Cirrus is a native Mac app for browsing, moving, syncing and backing up iCloud Drive and iCloud Photos — even on a Mac that isn't signed in to iCloud.

Two ways in

Works with the iCloud you already have.

Cirrus uses whichever iCloud is available, and can show several Apple IDs side by side.

💻

This Mac's iCloud

If your Mac is signed in, Cirrus uses it directly: iCloud Drive is the real folder macOS syncs, and Photos is your system library. Nothing extra to sign in to.

🔑

Any Apple ID, inside Cirrus

Not signed in on this Mac, or want a second account? Sign in the way iCloud.com does, with two-factor and Advanced Data Protection approval. Fill your login from the Passwords app; after that it's one Touch ID.

Features

Everything you expect from Finder.
Plus the parts iCloud never gave you.

🗂️

Finder-native browsing

Icon and list views, tabs (⌘T), two panes side by side (⌃⌘2), Quick Look with Space, Get Info, path bar, sorting and filtering.

🖐️

Drag anything anywhere

Between iCloud Drive, Photos, your disks and Finder. Dragging out of iCloud downloads on drop; dragging in uploads or imports.

🌄

Photos, properly

Your library grouped by month, albums and folders, Live Photos, favorites, Recently Deleted. Export originals with their capture dates.

📦

Managed transfers

A real queue with speed and time left. Size checks before anything starts, automatic retries, and your Mac stays awake while files move.

🔁

Sync jobs

Keep a folder copied, mirrored or two-way synced with iCloud Drive — on a schedule, or whenever a drive is plugged in. Always previewed first.

🛡️

Read-only mode

Hand it to anyone: browsing, downloading and backups work, but nothing in iCloud can change until you switch it off. Enforced in the engine, not just the buttons.

    Back Up iCloud

    A backup you can actually open.

    Pick a drive and Cirrus checks your iCloud against its free space, then downloads everything as ordinary files and folders: original photos with Live Photo videos, edits and RAW files, all dated by when they were taken. Later runs only fetch what's new, and it can run every time that drive is plugged in.

    Privacy & safety

    Your data goes to Apple. Nowhere else.

    Cirrus has no servers, no analytics and no accounts of its own. It's built so mistakes can be undone.

    iCloud ⇄ Google

    Leaving iCloud? Coming back?

    Move iCloud Drive and Photos to Google Drive and Google Photos, with albums, favorites and Live Photo videos. Or go the other way: bring Google Drive into iCloud Drive (Docs, Sheets and Slides become Word, Excel and PowerPoint files), and Google Photos from a Google Takeout export, with dates and albums intact.

    • Checks space on both sides before anything moves
    • Resumable and incremental: re-runs only send what's new
    • Never deletes your originals, and respects read-only mode
    • You connect with your own free Google Cloud client, so no third-party server ever sees your data

    FAQ

    Questions, answered.

    Is Cirrus made by Apple?

    No. Cirrus is an independent open-source project and isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Apple. It uses the same web services iCloud.com uses in your browser, or your Mac's own iCloud when the Mac is signed in.

    Does it need my Apple ID password?

    Only if your Mac isn't signed in to iCloud (or you add a second account). The password goes only to Apple, using the same secure sign-in (SRP) as iCloud.com, and two-factor authentication still applies. Cirrus keeps it only if you tick “Remember password” — in this Mac's Keychain, never synced, released only after Touch ID or your Mac password.

    What about Advanced Data Protection?

    It works. Turn on “Access iCloud Data on the Web” on your iPhone and approve the prompt Cirrus triggers, exactly like iCloud.com.

    Can it delete my stuff by accident?

    Deletions only ever move items to the Trash or to iCloud's Recently Deleted (recoverable for 30 days). Backups never write to iCloud, sync runs are previewed and stop if they'd remove an unusual share of files, replacing a file uploads the new one before retiring the old, and read-only mode blocks every change to iCloud.

    Where is my data stored?

    Your files stay in iCloud and wherever you copy them. Cirrus keeps thumbnails and recently opened files in your user Library with owner-only permissions; temporary copies are removed after a day and everything for an account is wiped when you sign out.

    Why do I need my own Google Cloud client?

    So your Google data goes straight between Google and your Mac, with no Cirrus server in the middle. Creating the client is free and takes a few minutes; Cirrus shows each step. Your Google sign-in stays in this Mac's Keychain and unlocks with Touch ID.

    Why does macOS warn me the first time I open it?

    Releases may not be notarized yet. Open it once with System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway. You can also build it yourself — it's one script.

    How big can files be?

    iCloud Drive stores files up to 50 GB. Uploads through iCloud's web services have been reported to stop around 10 GB, so Cirrus warns above that. FAT32 drives can't hold files over 4 GB, and Cirrus skips those with a clear message.
